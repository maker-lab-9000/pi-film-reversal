# Halation on the colour look

Date: 2026-10-17. Design: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-17-halation-design.md`.
Script: `scripts/experiments/halation_gallery.py`.

**Result: model C, the global mix with 2x headroom, strength 0.15, 0.25 mm.** The
owner chose it from two galleries. It is now a step of the grade
(`pifilm/halation.py`), off unless a look turns it on, and the look that has it is
`imx477-2026-09-v4-dark`.

## The question

On colour film, light passes through the emulsion, reflects off the film base and
comes back through the red-sensitive layer, the deepest one, so bright highlights
grow a soft red-orange glow. Can the grade add that so it reads as film, without
shifting the colour of the rest of the picture?

## The three models

All three work on the normalised frame in linear light, before the LUT (the look's
roll-off), with the glow's width in mm of a 36 mm frame, as the grain's is. Grain
goes on last, one seed per photograph, the same for every version of it.

- **A, threshold.** Only the highlights above a soft threshold (linear luminance
  0.70, about 0.85 in sRGB, with a smoothstep), blurred, tinted red-orange and added.
- **B, global mix.** A blurred copy of the whole frame mixed into red and a little
  green (weights 1.0, 0.35, 0.0), with no threshold. The blur is three Gaussians,
  `sigma`, `2 sigma` and `4 sigma` weighted 0.5, 0.3 and 0.2, about an exponential
  fall-off. A flat area equals its own blur, so it is unchanged and no colour shifts.
- **C, B with headroom.** The camera's JPEG has clipped its highlights, so a lamp is
  no brighter than white paper. Before the mix each value is expanded with
  `x / (1 - c x)` (white becomes `1 / (1 - c)`: 2x at c = 0.5, 4x at 0.75, 10x at
  0.9), and after it compressed back exactly with `y / (1 + c y)`.

The global, linear mix of a blurred copy rather than a highlight threshold, the
several blurs for an exponential-like fall-off, and the place before the print LUT
follow a published approach to film halation. The headroom expansion is ours,
because our input is a clipped JPEG and not a scan or a raw file.

## Round 1: the three models

15 photographs, at most 5 per folder, chosen for having some clipped highlights
(between 0.1 % and 8 % of the pixels near white), from `data/test-scenes-imx477`,
`data/source-imx477-v3-highlight` and `data/pi-2026-09-30` (four photographs appear
in both of the last two). Graded with `artifacts/imx477-2026-09-v4-dark-hp5-tile`,
the deployed look. Six versions of each:

| Version | Strength | Sigma | Headroom |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| off | | | |
| A threshold | 0.35 | 0.25 mm | |
| B global mix | 0.15 | 0.25 mm | |
| C mix + headroom | 0.08 | 0.25 mm | 10x (c = 0.9) |
| C mix + headroom | 0.15 | 0.25 mm | 10x |
| C mix + headroom | 0.3 | 0.4 mm | 10x |

What was seen:

- **A night lamp** (`2026-09-24_201359`). A barely shows. B turns the lamp
  blue-white: it pulls the red down inside the clipped area, where the blur is
  darker than the lamp, and puts little halo outside. C gives a warm glow and keeps
  the lamp white; the strong setting spreads orange into the dark wall.
- **A sunlit facade** (`pi-2026-09-30`, `150600`). C at 10x floods the dark windows
  with brown-orange even at 0.08, and with amber at the strong setting. A turns the
  wall cream. B is the mildest.

So the mix is the right shape (A tints whole bright areas; B and C leave flat areas
alone), headroom is what makes the glow show round a lamp, and 10x is too much on a
bright scene.

## Round 2: how much headroom

The owner's 8 selections from 2026-10-15 (originals `153220`, `153315`, `162330`,
`162422`, `164310`, `164520`, `164546`, `164629`, fetched from the Pi), with the
clip filter off, graded with the same look:

| Version | Strength | Sigma | Headroom |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| off | | | |
| B global mix | 0.15 | 0.25 mm | |
| C 2x | 0.15 | 0.25 mm | 2x (c = 0.5) |
| C 4x | 0.15 | 0.25 mm | 4x (c = 0.75) |
| C 4x narrow | 0.15 | 0.12 mm | 4x |
| C 10x | 0.08 | 0.25 mm | 10x (round 1's mildest) |

These are foliage against sky, and the changes are small: the gaps of sky between
the leaves soften with a faint warm edge, and the greens shift slightly olive. B, 2x
and 4x are hard to tell apart. 4x narrow keeps the leaf edges sharper. 10x is the
warmest. Nothing floods.

## The choice

C at 2x (headroom 0.5), strength 0.15, sigma 0.25 mm, weights 1.0, 0.35, 0.0: the
glow round a lamp without the facade's flood. These are the defaults of
`HalationParams` and of `scripts/stamp_halation.py`.

The look that gets it is `imx477-2026-09-v4-dark`, which has the deployed
`-hp5-tile` look's LUT (`98e3671`) and normalisation. Its Gaussian grain was turned
off so the GPU grades it, and it was stamped with the Pi's tuning
(`5404134af8256b674e5a8f93fe1bb616a1e0407e`). `stamp_halation` then raises it to
params version 5, so a build from before halation refuses it.

## How the step differs from the gallery

The step in `pifilm/halation.py` is model C, with three changes that move pixels by
a little and do not change the look:

- The frame is reduced once, by the whole factor that keeps two reduced pixels of
  the base sigma (`k = max(1, floor(sigma_px / 2))`: 3 on the 1014 px quick grade,
  14 at 4056 px), and the Gaussians are run on that copy. The script reduces
  separately for each of the three. The rule was four reduced pixels at first; it
  left the quick grade unreduced, and the quick grade is on the capture side, inside
  `shutter_to_ready_ms`. Against the three Gaussians run on the full frame the glow
  is within 6 % of its peak, a level after the mix at the chosen strength.
- The two wider Gaussians (2 and 4 sigma) are run on that copy reduced by a further
  2 and 4 (an area mean), at the same width, and read back with bilinear
  interpolation: within 2 % of the glow's peak of running them on the copy itself.
  On the Pi 4 the 4 sigma blur alone was most of the glow's time.
- Sigma in pixels is taken from the long side of the frame (`max(H, W)`); the script
  takes the width, which is the same for a landscape photograph.

## Rebuilding the galleries

The galleries are in `artifacts/halation-gallery/` and `artifacts/halation-gallery-2/`
(gitignored). The script's `VARIANTS` holds the round-2 set; model A's code is kept
for reference. Round 2:

```
.venv/bin/python scripts/experiments/halation_gallery.py --min-clip 0 --max-clip 1 \
  --per-folder 8 --artifacts artifacts/imx477-2026-09-v4-dark-hp5-tile \
  --out artifacts/halation-gallery-2 <folder of the eight originals>
```

Round 1 needs its own `VARIANTS` back in the script:

```python
VARIANTS = (
    Variant("off", "no halation"),
    Variant("threshold", "A threshold: highlights only, added, 0.35, 0.25 mm",
            "threshold", 0.35, 0.25),
    Variant("mix", "B global mix: 0.15, 0.25 mm", "mix", 0.15, 0.25),
    Variant("mixhr-subtle", "C global mix + headroom: 0.08, 0.25 mm", "mix", 0.08, 0.25, 0.9),
    Variant("mixhr", "C global mix + headroom: 0.15, 0.25 mm", "mix", 0.15, 0.25, 0.9),
    Variant("mixhr-strong", "C global mix + headroom: 0.3, 0.4 mm", "mix", 0.3, 0.4, 0.9),
)
```

```
.venv/bin/python scripts/experiments/halation_gallery.py --per-folder 5 \
  --artifacts artifacts/imx477-2026-09-v4-dark-hp5-tile --out artifacts/halation-gallery \
  data/test-scenes-imx477 data/source-imx477-v3-highlight data/pi-2026-09-30
```
