# Ilford HP5 Plus 400 grain, measured from scans

Offline experiment, 2026-10-01. Nothing in production changes: `pifilm/grain.py`
and every artifact keep their grain. The question was whether HP5 Plus 400's grain
can be measured from public scans and redrawn on IMX477 frames, as a model to fit
instead of the three hand-set numbers in `GrainParams` (strength 0.004, one
0.7 px blur, a fixed `4Y(1-Y)` envelope).

Code: `pifilm/experiments/grain.py`, tests in `tests/test_experiment_grain.py`.

```sh
.venv/bin/python -m pifilm.experiments.grain data/hp5-commons --out artifacts/hp5-grain
```

## Corpus

Wikimedia Commons, *Category:Taken on Ilford HP5 plus 400*: 136 files, 135 with
an accepted licence (CC BY, CC BY-SA, CC0). Grain only survives a scan that
resolves it, so files under 3400 px on the long side (about 2400 dpi for 35 mm)
were left out. That removed 80 of one author's 2048 px flatbed scans. The 45
remaining originals were downloaded at full resolution into `data/hp5-commons/`
(gitignored), with `manifest.json` and `ATTRIBUTION.md`.

`pifilm-fetch` could not be used. Its validation rejects greyscale images, which
is right for colour references and wrong for black-and-white film. It also
downloads Commons thumbnails, which are capped at 3840 px and re-encoded. A
one-off script reused its listing and licence checks and fetched the originals,
six seconds apart, because Commons answered faster requests with HTTP 429.

From a contact sheet and 1:1 crops, 10 were excluded: one oversharpened, one
toned print, two sepia scans that were the smoothest in the set despite the
highest pixel counts, and six that looked noise-reduced or sharpened. 35 were
measured.

## Method

Grain is read only from flat patches (128 px tiles). A tile counts as flat when
its low-pass content varies less than 0.35 times its high-pass residual, so
grass, bark and brick are skipped. The residual (the tile minus a Gaussian blur
of 8 px) is the grain. From it:

- **Strength against brightness:** the residual's standard deviation, binned by
  the patch's mean display luminance.
- **Clump size:** the radially averaged power spectrum, fitted with white noise
  through Gaussian filters, converted to millimetres on the film using the frame
  width (36 mm).

## Results

**Scan resolution limits what can be seen.** The fitted clump size tracks the
scanner, not the film:

| Source | Scans | px per mm | One-Gaussian clump σ | Mid-tone sd at scan |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kai Burghardt (lossless PNG) | 4 | 155 | 7.2 µm | 0.039–0.075 |
| Pierre Marshall | 1 | 149 | 4.6 µm | 0.048 |
| UnreifeKirsche | 26 | 97 | 16.1 µm | 0.036 |

UnreifeKirsche's spectrum drops quickly to a flat floor: the scan blurs the
grain and adds scanner and JPEG noise. Only the two sharpest sources are used
for clump size.

**One clump size is the wrong model.** Kai's spectrum falls off gradually, which
a single Gaussian cannot follow: the fit put 45% of the variance into its noise
floor, and grain synthesised from it was visibly blobbier than the scan.
Fine plus coarse clumps match:

| | Value |
|---|---|
| Fine clump σ | 4.3 µm (0.49 px at the IMX477's 112.7 px per mm) |
| Coarse clump σ | 11.2 µm (1.26 px) |
| Fine share of grain variance | 76% |

Kai's four scans agree closely: fine 4.1–4.7 µm, coarse 9.1–11.7 µm, fine
share 69–88%. Pierre Marshall's single scan did not separate the two scales.

**Strength against brightness** (pooled from Kai's scans, relative to
mid-grey): strongest in the upper mid-tones, weaker in shadows, and falling
off in highlights. It does not follow the production envelope's symmetric
`4Y(1-Y)` hump.

| Display luminance | 0.05 | 0.15 | 0.25 | 0.35 | 0.45 | 0.55 | 0.65 | 0.75 | 0.85 | 0.95 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Relative sd | 0.25 | 0.35 | 0.56 | 0.63 | 1.02 | 0.98 | 1.14 | 1.04 | 0.62 | 0.36 |

**Absolute strength varies between scans** by almost 2× (mid-tone sd 0.039–0.075
in Kai's four scans), most likely from development and scan contrast. The pooled
curve is dominated by the grainiest scan, which has the most flat patches, so
strength is left as a choice. Redrawn on the camera grid, by generating
grain at 155 px per mm over a 36 mm frame and resizing to 4056 px, a unit field
keeps 0.84 of its standard deviation. A simple pixel-pitch ratio would predict
0.73, because the coarse clumps survive the resize. The typical scan's strength
on the IMX477 is therefore about 0.040 at mid-grey; production uses 0.004.

## Renders

`artifacts/hp5-grain/hp5-grain-1to1.png` and `artifacts/hp5-grain/renders/`
(gitignored) show four v4-dark frames at 0.004 (current), 0.025, 0.040 and
0.060. At 1:1, 0.025 is already plainly visible, 0.040 reads as HP5, and
0.060 is gritty. One side effect: the fine grain dithers away the 8-bit banding
that is visible on smooth gradients with the current grain, such as the DHL
van's yellow side.

## Open

- A pipeline version needs `params.json` grain fields for two clump sizes in
  millimetres, the fine share, the relative curve and a strength, plus a Pi
  timing check. Generating at scan resolution and resizing costs about 23 MP of
  blur per shot, so the Pi should draw directly on the camera grid with the 0.84
  correction.
- Sensor noise at high gain adds to synthetic grain on night frames. It is not
  subtracted yet.
- HP5 is a black-and-white film. Its grain on a colour look is a creative pairing,
  not an emulation.
